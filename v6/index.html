What it refuses to do

Most tools will write anything you point them at. These five rules are the reason ours will not — and they are the same five whether you have told us everything or almost nothing.

1

Every claim traces to something you told us

If a sentence says something about your restaurant — an ingredient, a turnaround, a qualification — it points at the answer it came from, or it does not ship.

2

Missing facts get asked for, never inferred

When it needs something it does not have, it writes you a question instead of a guess. One batched question a week.

3

“Best”, “award-winning” and “most loved” need a source

A superlative only survives if it traces to a real review or a real award you gave us.

4

No prices unless you marked the price publishable

Ever. The most common way a food account embarrasses itself is an out-of-date price.

5

The compliance list is a hard block, not a warning

Halal, allergen and licensing language is refused outright unless your own answer supports it. We do not upgrade “halal ingredients” to “halal-certified”.

3 beliefs that guide our company

Everyone else promises you everything. We do not.

Suara will tell you it does not know your licence, your certification, or your claim. That refusal is the product. A tool that never says no will eventually say something untrue about your restaurant.

Restaurants deserve the tech the chains already have.

A chain has a content team. You have a Tuesday night. Suara is the part of that team you can actually afford, and it works the same way every week.

Your voice is yours. We only keep it.

Nothing here is a template with your name dropped in. It learns the way you write, and every correction you make is kept for good.
